# QUESTION_STYLE — CSC 583

## The real exam (from the student; no past papers available)
- Written paper, about **5 questions**. There **may be a compulsory Question 1**. Candidates answer **3 or 4** in total.
- Duration and marks are not known. **Assume 2 hours and ~20 marks per question** (parts a, b, c, optionally d), shown as `<span class="marks">(n marks)</span>`.
- Most marks test **concepts exactly as taught**: define, list, state, explain, differentiate, draw. Some parts are **scenario-based application**.

## How site questions are written
- **Recall first:** every unit's Recall drill (STRUCTURE.md override 3) covers every definable/listable item. Sample exam questions should be answerable from the courseware.
- **Sample exam questions (3–4 per unit):**
    - Q-type A, "recall-heavy": (a) define/state (4–6), (b) list and explain N items (6–8), (c) differentiate X and Y on named criteria (6–8).
    - Q-type B, "applied": (a) recall (4–6), (b) explain/compare (6–8), (c) **scenario** (6–10): a believable case (a Nigerian bank, university, hospital, fintech, government agency…) where the student must identify the concept, classify, recommend and **justify, including why not the alternatives** (I-A-J-A method).
    - Use courseware numbers and lists directly (e.g. "the four categories of system assets", "the three impact levels").
    - Use the command words the examiner uses: *define, state, list, explain, describe, differentiate/distinguish, discuss, outline, with the aid of a diagram*.
- **Model answers** as a strong student would write them: short definition first; then points each with a **bold heading** and 1–3 sentences; examples where the question asks for application; a **Marking points** line at the end whose marks add up to the question's marks.
- **Compulsory Q1 style (used in mock exams):** many short parts across units (e.g. 8 × 2–3 marks of definitions and "state two…").
- **MCQs** are study aids only (the exam probably has none): key facts + traps, plausible distractors from neighbouring concepts, explanations that teach and name the section.
